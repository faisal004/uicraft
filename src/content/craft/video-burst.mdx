## What is a video burst?

A video burst is a hero video that comes apart as you scroll past it. The video isn't playing in a normal `<video>` element on the page. It's drawn as a grid of small square tiles, each one painting its own piece of the current frame. Lined up, the tiles are the video. When you scroll, they let go: the middle first, then out towards the edges, each tile flying off, swirling, floating up, and shrinking into a round dot that keeps the colour of the footage under it.

Because the scroll position decides everything, it runs backwards too. Scroll up and the dots fly home and the picture closes up again.

## How this video burst works

1. **Hide the video and use it as a texture.** A muted, looping `<video>` plays off screen. Every frame that changes is uploaded to WebGL as a texture. The clip is one slow camera pan, so it was encoded forwards and then backwards: the camera drifts right, glides back, and ends one frame before where it started, so the loop has no visible jump.
2. **Cover the hero with tiles.** JavaScript lays a grid of 7 px squares over the canvas and gives each one four random numbers. That grid goes into one buffer, and it's rebuilt only when the hero changes size.
3. **Paint each tile with its piece of the video.** Each tile is drawn as a WebGL point. Every pixel inside a point reads the video at its own spot, so neighbouring tiles join up into the full picture. The crop matches `object-fit: cover`.
4. **Send one number per frame.** The scroll position becomes a progress value from 0 to 1. The vertex shader gives each tile its own slice of that range, starting earlier in the middle and later at the edges.
5. **Fly, swirl, lift, and come forward.** Within its slice, a tile moves away from the centre with an ease-out, rotates around the middle, drifts up, and is pushed towards or away from you so near dots spread and grow.
6. **Turn tiles into dots.** As a tile flies, its spread over the video shrinks to a single colour and its square edge fades to a soft circle.

Nothing is simulated, so there's nothing to get out of sync. Progress 0 is always the video and progress 1 is always the cloud.

## Things to try

- Scroll a little and watch the hole open in the middle while the edges hold.
- Stop halfway. The loose dots keep fluttering and keep changing colour with the footage.
- Scroll back to the top and watch the picture close up.
- Click the hero and use the arrow keys or Page Down to scroll it.
- Open the [full page demo](/demo/video-burst), where the page scroll drives the burst.

With reduced motion turned on, the video holds on its first frame and loose dots don't flutter. Scrolling still takes it apart, since you're the one moving it.
